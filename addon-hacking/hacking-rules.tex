	\chapter{On Hacking in this book}
	The following hacking rules are in replacement of the ones in the core rules.
	This is to give them more depth and make them more of a focal point mechanically.
	\par
    Hacking is magic, in the broadest sense:
    It requires rare sorcerers to perform,
    and it greatly shifts the very fragile balance of power.
    \\%
    These people will be sought after and hunted by criminal syndicates and greedy corps alike,
    and have to be ever vigilant.
    \par
	\begin{exampleblock}
		This book will refer to \emph{\modifiedlincname}.
		This is a slang term for a wide variety of modified L1NC implants,
			that specifically pass the usual safety measures
			- in exchange it allows an experienced hacker to modify the AR around them.
	\end{exampleblock}
	
	\chapter{Types of attacks}
	\section{AR Attacks}
	Attacking the AR supplements the AR with wrong information to display.
	These attacks can be targeted either at one person or at every person in the area.
	\par
	These attacks are fast and only require a wireless connection to the AR using a \modifiedlincname,
		but they can only modify the display.
	At most these attacks can blind or deafen people for a moment.
	Usually they produce illusions, share information or control traffic.
	They may also track every \emph{standard} L1NC in the vicinity - other hackers are generally immune against this simple ping.
	\\%
	If they last too long, authorities will notice and begin to track the user.
	\section{Deep Attacks}
	Deep Attacks target services and systems that aren't directly controlled by the AR.
	\par
	They require a physical connection and a decent amount of experience.
	They are, however, also much more powerful, as they can grant access to devices.
	This may allow opening electronic door locks, hijacking camera feeds, or disabling sirens.
	\section{Deep Dive}
	A Deep Dive is a long-form Deep Attack.
	The goal is to enter the service through the Deep from a remote location.
	\par
	They require a lot of equipment and experience to perform, and they are very risky.
	\\%
	Goals are plentiful and reach from corporate espionage to sabotage by denial of service.
	\begin{exampleblock}
		\itshape
		GM Note:
		\\%
		Dives are lengthy processes.
		The rules are meant to be used as set pieces or whole missions themselves.
	\end{exampleblock}

	\chapter{AR Attacks}
	\section{Prerequisites}
	To perform AR Attacks, the character needs a \modifiedlincname\ and the necessary experience with AR technology,
		represented by the ability \emph{Misinformer} (p. \pageref{ability:misinformer}).
	Experience in \emph{\hackingskill} obviously does not hurt.
	\section{Goals}
	AR Attacks can achieve two things:
	\paragraph{Targeted Effects}
		A single person is targeted to suffer some effect.
		\\%
		The user makes a \emph{\hackingskill} test.
		The target opposes the test using \emph{Perception}.
		The attack takes 2 actions to perform.
		\\%
		The larger and more detailed the illusion is supposed to be, the harder it will be to create.
		For more details see section \ref{sec:arspellbook} below.
	\paragraph{Area Effects}
		All L1NCs in the area are affected by the user's hack.
		\\%
		The user makes a \emph{\hackingskill} test.
		Everyone in the vicinity may oppose the test using \emph{Perception}
			- on a success they recognize the illusion as misinformation and are essentially unaffected.
		\\%
		Effecting an area is much harder and suffers a -20.
		The attack also now takes 4 actions to perform.

	\chapter{Deep Attacks}
	\section{Prerequisites}
	The user needs a \modifiedlincname\ and the necessary experience to dive, represented by the ability \emph{Diver I} (p. \pageref{ability:diveri}).
	In addition any Deep Attack requires a physical connection
		- either through an external device
		or an implant (e.g. a Subdermal Jack, p. \pageref{implant:subdermaljack}).
	\section{Goals}
	The target of such an attack is a single service or closed system, such as door controls or a surveillance system.
	The goal can be to open or close, activate or deactivate, or hijack and control the system.
	\\%
    Performing a deep attack requires an action to connect (possibly in conjunction with a \emph{Grappling} test),
    and an action to perform the attack - requiring a \emph{\hackingskill} test at a given penalty (see below).

	\chapter{Deep Dive}
	\section{Prerequisites}
	A Deep Dive requires a \emph{diving rig} (see chapter \ref{ch:rigs}) and the necessary experience to navigate the Deep, represented by \emph{Diver II} (p. \pageref{ability:diverii}).
	If a group dives together, at least one needs the ability, but the other may follow along and act as long as their leader is still with them.
	\section{Sensation}
	Services in the Deep are translated into human sensations.
	This means any service will appear like an area that its creator is familiar with
		- e.g. office buildings, cave systems, or medieval fantasy marketplaces.
	\section{Goals}
	\paragraph{Attackers}
		enter a service, looking for a representation of their target.
		They interact with this target in a way that mimics what they want to achieve.
		\begin{exampleblock}
			Our protagonists enter a security service in search for a database,
				which they find in form of a file drawer.
			Within this drawer they find their likenesses and toss them into a shredder.
			They are then removed from this security service's database.
		\end{exampleblock}
	\paragraph{ACM}
		- \emph{Automated Counter Measures} - are the native defense mechanism of any service.
		ACMs have a level of alertness.
		The more anomalies (bugs or intrusions) they identify, the slower the service will become,
			as more resources are diverted to the ACMs.
		\\%
		ACMs usually appear to divers as cameras, scanners, turrets and other automated defense systems.
		The higher the alertness, the more of these devices will appear where they weren't previously.
		\\%
		ACMs look to identify,
			and either "physically" remove the intruders from the premises
            or completely neutralize them to cancel their connection.
		Advanced ACMs may radically shift the environment to do so.
		\begin{exampleblock}
			\itshape
			Kid and Shiv attempt to break into a local news provider.
			Initially things go smoothly,
				but while trying to plant false info,
				they make a mistake and get picked up.
			As the ACM increases in alertness,
				the service's normal process slows down visibly,
				lots of cameras show up in the simulation,
				and a security diver is en route.
		\end{exampleblock}
	\paragraph{Defenders}
		roam a service looking for both bugs as well as intruders, trying to locate and neutralize them.
		Divers can patrol areas that ACMs cannot easily cover, and make intuitive decisions on how to approach different anomalies.
		\\%
		A service with one or a team of multiple divers on standby will be alerted by the ACM at some point.
		Corpo systems are generally always staffed.
		\\%
		Just like an intruder, a defender can also be "neutralized".
		He is not booted from the service for good, however, and may reconnect after a short while
			- giving the intruders a small time frame to act.
	\section{Combat}
    \subsection*{Preparation}
    When entering the Deep Net,
        divers may bring one melee and one ranged weapon with them
        - these are obviously not actual weapons but representations of aggressive code fragments.
    Depending on the type of code, the weapon will change,
        i.e. larger weapons are easily detectable attacks by ACMs and other divers,
        but deal more damage if they hit.
    \subsection*{Encounter}
    A combat encounter for untrained divers works just like in reality.
    For trained divers, however, things work somewhat differently.
    \\%
    All divers who have the \emph{Diver II} ability gain an additional action per combat round.
    They can use this action to shift the environment.
    For possible effects and penalties refer to the AR and Deep spellbooks.
    \\%
    Defenders gain +20 to these required tests.
    They also may alert the ACM, which they don't have to roll for at all.

	\chapter{Building a Rig}
	\label{ch:rigs}
	\section{Energy Limits}
	Powering a massive rig is not possible everywhere.
	More importantly, booting up a rig causes a massive power spike in the grid, very noticeable on surveillance.
	This may put the divers on a time crunch that's much tighter than acceptable.
	\par
	While in civilization, once the energy demand surpasses 2, surveillance systems will slowly start to pick up on the rig as an unknown anomaly.
	Anyone who can take readings of the power grid will take notice when energy demand reaches 6, and can guess that it's a rig.
	The surveyors' reactions can vary widely.
	\begin{exampleblock}
		\itshape
		Technically space can be a limiting factor, as some components may be quite large.
		Considering how much size varies between components, this limit will not receive numbers.
	\end{exampleblock}
	\section{Access Point}
	The rig's heart is an access point.
	It translates the Deep into human-understandable sensations, and the human mind into instructions.
	Divers need a physical connection to this device to use it.
	\section{Bench}
	A diver has to lay down to prevent falling over.
	While technically the floor is viable, spasms during the dive can hurt.
	Special ergonomic benches can mitigate some of that damage,
		while being submerged in a nutrient vat not only reduces injury risk but can also extend the maximum time of the dive.
	\section{Peripherals}
	Further hardware may be attached to the access point to alter the behavior during the dive.
	More powerful access points - while requiring more energy - can support more peripherals.
