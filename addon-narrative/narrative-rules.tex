	\chapter{Introduction}
	Some rules may be added depending on the type of game and the group playing. On the following pages you will find a collection of optional narrative rules.
	\par
	These are adaptations of rules in other game systems,
		purely optional and made as a last way out of dead ends in extremely desperate situations
		or to reward Voices for extraordinary role play.
	\par
	It is encouraged for the Adjudicator to keep them at hand and to offer them in dire or epic situations to keep fun and motivation up, or to restore hope.

	\chapter{Aces of your sleeve}
	\nrule{I know a guy}{
		When the Crew has absolutely no idea where to continue,
			a critical Cooperator died in unfortunate circumstances
			or something similar,
			a Voice may exclaim “I know a guy”.
	}{
		The Voice in question describes a Bystander.
		This description includes a name, a profession, how he would be able to help and why… or at what cost.
		The Adjudicator should decide on some sort of side effect.
		Maybe the Bystander is currently wanted and the Crew will be targeted by association
			or the Protagonist is in the Bystander’s bad books for one reason or another.
		He may have been mentioned in the Protagonist's 20 questions before, though this should not be required.\\
		Without any caveat the use of this rule should always be denied;
			if a Voice is looking for friends in high places, a boon exists for that,
			or the Crew will actually have to put in the work.}
	\nrule{A monkey's paw}{A Protagonist just scored a bare failure but really needs to succeed.}{
		The test may be allowed to pass - at a cost.
		This may be taking a hit to health, exhaustion or reputation;
			alternatively equipment may be damaged among many other, more situational complications
			like being detected.
		The more sense this consequence makes in narration, the better.\\
		Both the Adjudicator and the Voice should consent to the effect.}
	\nrule{Unique experience}{
		Mechanically a Voice passes an extremely difficult test.
		Narratively a Protagonist lives through a trying, mind breaking and personality altering ordeal against all odds.}{
		The Protagonist may gain a 50\% discount on one level of a skill or education, or to an ability.
		Alternatively a disabling characteristic may be reduced by 5 points due to the newly found courage or the change in perspective.}
	\nrule{Loyalty is its own reward}{A Protagonist succumbs to fear or a Disabling Characteristic,
			causing infighting or complete and total derailing of the mission or campaign.}{
		The Protagonist may, with permission of the Adjudicator, declare loyalty to the Crew.
		He may act to protect and support the Crew
			as if he was not affected
			but in turn forfeits his own decision making.\\
		As Banes and Fear exist for a reason, it is advised to allow this sparingly and in critical situations only.}
	\nrule{The Finest Hour}{Once per Protagonist (or Antagonist...) ever, in the most dire of situations.}{
		The Subject immediately takes a turn in combat,
			which does not require a declaration phase.
		This may be right after his own.
		This cannot interrupt another Subject.}
